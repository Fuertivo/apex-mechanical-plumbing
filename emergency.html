Before We Arrive

Four things to do in the first two minutes.

These steps limit damage while a technician is en route. If you are unsure, the dispatcher will stay on the phone and walk you through them.

  1. Shut off the main valve. Usually at the meter, in a utility closet or where the service line enters the house. Turn clockwise until it stops.
  2. Kill power to wet areas. If water is near outlets, appliances or a panel, shut the breaker off — only if you can reach it without standing in water.
  3. Contain and document. Towels, buckets, a wet/dry vac. Photograph everything before cleanup for your insurance file.
  4. Move people and valuables. Clear the work area, secure pets, and move furniture or electronics off wet flooring.

What Counts As An Emergency

If it is on this list, call now.

When in doubt, call. A dispatcher would rather tell you it can wait until morning than have you discover a soaked ceiling at 6 AM.

Priority 1

Burst pipe or active flooding

Water escaping a pipe, fixture or appliance. Shut the main off if you can, then call. We dispatch immediately.

Priority 1

Sewage backup into the home

Stop using water and avoid contact with waste. This is a health hazard and receives immediate routing.

Priority 1

No water to the whole house

If neighbors have water and you do not, the issue is likely on your side of the meter. We will help you confirm.

Priority 2

No hot water

Same-day priority during operating hours. Overnight dispatch is available when the failure involves an active leak.

Priority 2

Water heater leaking

Shut off the cold inlet and the gas valve or breaker, then call. A leaking tank rarely improves on its own.

Priority 2

A leak you can hear in a wall

Shut the main off to stop the damage and call. We will isolate and locate before opening the wall.
